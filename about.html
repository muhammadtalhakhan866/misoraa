Our Story

The Long Way Round.

We started by taking watches apart, not by designing them.

The first year of MISORAA produced nothing to sell. It was spent on a single bench, opening up other people's watches to understand where corners get cut — the bridges left unfinished because nobody will look, the gaskets specified a grade too thin, the plating measured in marketing rather than microns.

What came out of that year was a short list of things we would not do. The list is still on the wall of the atelier, and it is still shorter than our specification sheet. Every reference we have released since has had to pass it before it got a name.

The result is a house that moves slowly. Six references in seven years, each produced in a run small enough that one watchmaker can take a piece from first screw to final polish — and sign for it.

Craftsmanship

What Goes In,
Before It Goes On.

Four standards every reference has to meet before it is allowed to carry the mark.

01

The Calibre

Swiss-grade automatic movements, regulated in five positions and tested over 14 days before a case ever closes around them.

02

The Finish

18K gold PVD over surgical steel, applied in six passes — a finish measured in microns, not marketing claims.

03

The Seal

Every case is pressure-tested individually. Screw-down crowns, triple gaskets, and a caseback torqued to spec by hand.

04

The Promise

An 18-month international warranty, lifetime servicing at cost, and a certificate numbered to your piece alone.

Inside The Atelier

Forty-Two Steps.

A MISORAA passes through forty-two hand operations between the parts tray and the presentation case. Nineteen of them are finishing: bevelling bridge edges, graining the mainplate, polishing screw heads to a black mirror that most owners will never see.

None of it makes the watch keep better time. All of it is the difference between a watch that is assembled and a watch that is made.
